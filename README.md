## What the $1.99 plan actually buys

The bottleneck on VPS Promo is the **10 Mbps fair-share port**, not the traffic allowance. A 10 Mbps ceiling works out to roughly 1.25 MB/s at full speed. A text-heavy page under 100 KB is fine; a product page with a few unoptimised photos is not, because two or three concurrent visitors can saturate the link and everyone waits. JustHost's own footnote puts the equivalent monthly traffic at "4+ TB", which is what you'd move if the port ran flat out for the entire month. Real sites never do, and the shared port means your neighbours' usage affects what you actually get.

512 MB of RAM is the second constraint. That is enough for a static site, a reverse proxy, a monitoring agent or a WireGuard endpoint. It is not enough for a WordPress install with a page builder, WooCommerce, or a database plus a control panel. If any of those words describe your plan, the $1.99 tier is a false economy.

## The real monthly cost: prepay price vs. what you'll pay later

Every price in those tables is a 12-month prepay rate, and JustHost shows the undiscounted list price next to it. That part is transparent. What the price page does **not** do is publish a separate renewal figure, so the number that applies in month 13 has to be confirmed on the checkout screen before you pay. Budget for the list price rather than the promo price and you won't get a surprise: Sirius Special renewing at $2.81 is still cheap; Altair Special renewing at $47.40 is a different commitment.

Add-ons are where cheap VPS bills drift upward. A worked example from a competitor shows the size of the effect: Hostinger includes only weekly backups free, charges **$3.00 a month** for daily ones, which adds **$64.80** across a 24-month plan, and lifts the renewal price to $9.99 a month. A $5.84 headline becomes something else entirely once you add daily backups and the second-term rate.

The formula worth running before checkout:

**First-year cost = (prepay price × 12) + (each paid add-on × 12) + one-off setup fees**

Common line items to check on any budget VPS: control panel licences (cPanel, Plesk and DirectAdmin are almost always extra), automatic backups or snapshots, additional IPv4 addresses, managed support, migration help, and malware scanning or advanced DDoS filtering. Liquid Web charges separately for cPanel; Hostwinds doesn't include an SSL certificate and gives you three days to change your mind. None of this makes a host dishonest, but it does mean the cheapest advertised plan is rarely the cheapest working server.

For JustHost specifically, confirm two things at checkout: whether snapshots or backups are included in the plan you picked, and the amount billed when the 12 months are up. Their published refund terms settle on a pro-rata basis for the unused part of the service period rather than advertising a fixed "30-day money-back" window, so a short first term is the cheaper way to test the platform.

## "Unmetered" traffic and the footnotes behind it

JustHost labels every port "unmetered (fair-share)" and then gives the theoretical monthly equivalent: 4+ TB at 10 Mbps, 31+ TB at 100 Mbps, 62+ TB at 200 Mbps, 93+ TB at 300 Mbps, 155+ TB at 500 Mbps, 232+ TB at 750 Mbps. Those numbers are arithmetic, not a promise of sustained throughput. Fair-share means the port capacity is divided among the VMs on the host, so a busy neighbour at 8 pm changes your speed.

This is an industry habit, not a JustHost quirk. OVHcloud advertises unlimited traffic on its Singapore VPS range and then states the actual monthly quota in the footnote: **500 GB on VPS-1, 1 TB on VPS-2 and VPS-3, 3 TB on VPS-4**, with bandwidth capped at **10 Mbps** once you exceed it. Same page, two very different readings of "unlimited".

Before you buy any cheap VPS, find the answers to these:

1. Is the traffic number a quota, or a theoretical maximum derived from port speed?
2. What happens after you exceed it — overage charge, throttle, or suspension?
3. Is the port dedicated or shared, and is the quoted speed a burst or a guarantee?
4. Is there a fair-use clause that lets the host throttle "abusive" usage at its discretion?

**Who shouldn't buy a VPS at all.** If you're still learning what FTP and a document root are, a managed shared plan will cost less in time than a $2 server you have to secure yourself. If your site is a low-traffic brochure page that nobody depends on, shared hosting is fine. If you have no interest in patching a kernel, configuring a firewall or reading logs, an unmanaged VPS is the wrong purchase regardless of price. The usual trigger for moving up is traffic past roughly 90,000 monthly visits, a need for root access or custom PHP modules, a development environment, or handling payments and personal data.

## FAQ

**Will the price go up at renewal?**
JustHost lists a 12-month prepay price next to an undiscounted list price, but doesn't publish a separate renewal rate on the pricing pages. Check the renewal amount at checkout and assume the list price rather than the promo price when you budget.

**Is 1 GB RAM enough for WordPress?**
For one site with a caching plugin, a lightweight theme and modest traffic, yes. Add WooCommerce, a page builder and a control panel, and 1 GB becomes tight fast. Move to 2 GB.

**Can a cheap VPS run WooCommerce?**
Yes, from 4 GB of RAM upward. Vega Special at $6.23/month gives you 2 cores, 4 GB and 80 GB NVMe, which is a realistic floor for a small store. Below that you'll spend more time tuning MySQL than selling.

**Do I need cPanel?**
Only if you want a graphical interface for email, DNS and databases. On budget VPS plans a cPanel licence usually costs extra each month, and it consumes RAM you'd otherwise give to your site.

**Is a $1.99 VPS reliable enough to use?**
For a static site, a proxy or a test box, yes. For a store or anything handling customer data, spend the extra 26 cents and take the 1 GB plan with the 300 Mbps port, then keep your own off-server backups regardless of what the host promises.
